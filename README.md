## What This Does

A writer's text goes through seven steps before a reader sees a label.

1. **Arrives.** `POST /submit` receives JSON with `text` and `creator_id` (`app.py::submit`).
2. **Validated.** Missing, empty, or non-string `text` or `creator_id` returns HTTP 400. Text over 20,000 characters returns HTTP 413. Rejected requests write nothing to the log.
3. **Signal one.** `detector.model_signal` asks a local gpt2 model how predictable the text is and returns a 0–1 score (higher = more AI-like).
4. **Signal two.** `stylometry.style_signal` measures the shape of the text (sentence-length spread, word variety, punctuation) and returns a 0–1 score in the same direction.
5. **Combined.** `scoring.combine_signals` turns the two scores into one number.
6. **Labelled.** `scoring.score_to_label` maps that number to one of three labels and a short `guess` (`ai`, `human`, `unsure`).
7. **Logged and answered.** `audit.log_decision` writes both signal scores, the combined score, the label and a `decided` status to `logs/audit.jsonl`, and the response returns the same fields plus a `content_id`.

If a writer disagrees, `POST /appeal` takes the `content_id` and their reasoning, writes the appeal to the log next to the original decision, and moves the item to `under_review`. An unknown `content_id` returns HTTP 404 and writes nothing.

## Signals and Scoring

### Signal one — the model signal

**What it measures:** how predictable the text is to a small language model (gpt2), as perplexity, scaled to a 0–1 score. File: `detector.py`.

**Why that might differ between human and AI writing:** text generated by a language model tends to use the most likely next word, so another model finds it easy to predict. Human writing tends to surprise the model more.

**What it can't see:** meaning, intent, or who wrote it. It only sees predictability, so plain, everyday human writing also looks predictable. In my test sets, casual human posts scored very AI-like (h15 0.83, h12 0.80, h10 0.78), while rhymed AI poems scored low (a09 0.18, a07 0.29). It is also noisy on short texts of about 80–100 words.

### Signal two — the style signal

**What it measures:** the shape of the text, without reading it. File: `stylometry.py`. It combines three measures, each scaled to 0–1 and flipped so that higher means more AI-like: sentence-length spread (standard deviation divided by mean, weight 0.4), type-token ratio over the first 100 words (weight 0.3), and punctuation marks per word (weight 0.3). A single sentence has no spread, so that measure is skipped.

**Why that might differ between human and AI writing:** generated prose tends toward even, mid-length sentences, a narrower vocabulary, and tidy punctuation. Human writing is lumpier.

**What it can't see:** what the text says. Anything that changes the shape without changing the substance moves it: typos, chopped-up sentences, pasted quotations. Poems are also a weak spot, because the sentence splitter looks for full stops and poem lines often have none. In my test sets it barely separated the groups (AI pieces 0.39–0.52, human pieces 0.26–0.58).

### The combining rule

**The rule:** a weighted average of the two scores. If the two signals differ by more than 0.35, the result is pulled halfway back toward 0.5. Two methods disagreeing is itself uncertainty, so an "AI" verdict needs both signals leaning the same way. I chose this to protect writers: a wrongly flagged human costs more than a missed AI piece.

**The numbers:** model weight 0.5, style weight 0.5, disagreement gap 0.35, shrink factor 0.5 (all in `config.py`). Label cutoffs: human at or below 0.35, AI at or above 0.90.

**Where it lives:** `scoring.py::combine_signals`

**A case where my two signals split, and what my rule does with it:** piece h10, a casual post asking for movie recommendations (human-written). Signal one gave 0.781 and signal two gave 0.329, a gap of 0.45. A plain average is 0.555. Because the gap is over 0.35 the result is pulled to about 0.53, firmly "unsure" instead of leaning AI.

### What my test sets showed

I wrote my criteria first (`criteria.md`, committed before any scores), then scored 20 human pieces (`tests/human.jsonl`) and 10 AI pieces (`tests/ai.jsonl`).

- Signal one: human median 0.565, AI median 0.536. Signal two: human 0.423, AI 0.457. Combined scores ran from about 0.28 to 0.70.
- **Criterion 1 (no human labelled AI): met, but trivially.** With the cutoff at 0.9, no piece of either kind reaches the AI label.
- **Criterion 2 (median gap of 0.4): missed by a wide margin.** The combined medians were about 0.48 for human and 0.48 for AI. The two signals together do not separate these samples.
- **Criterion 3 (label coverage): partly missed.** The mapping works at the boundaries, but no real input reaches the AI label. I also set the human cutoff at 0.35, not the 0.2 written in the criterion, because the lowest real score was about 0.28 and 0.2 would make the human label unreachable too.
- **Criteria 4 and 5 (appeal path, bad input): met in testing.** Appeals set `under_review` and log the previous status; an unknown id returns 404; missing text returns 400; 20,001 characters returns 413 and 20,000 passes.

Caveat: all three human poems (Eliot, Donne, Frost) are famous and may be memorized by gpt2, and all four AI poems are rhymed quatrains, so the poem comparison is weak.

## Label Variants

| Label | Score range | The exact text a reader sees |
|---|---|---|
| high-confidence human | 0.00 – 0.35 | This text may be human-written, but we cannot verify the authorship. No further action is required. |
| unsure | 0.35 – 0.90 | We could not determine whether AI was used. No action is required, and the writer can request a review. |
| high-confidence AI | 0.90 – 1.00 | This text may be AI-generated, but this can be wrong. The writer can request a review. |

**Why I worded the "unsure" one this way:** it does not accuse anyone and it says plainly that the system could not decide. It also tells the writer they can ask for a review, because the person reading it may have written every word themselves. Even the "human" label says it cannot verify authorship, because the system cannot prove it either way.

## How I Used AI

I used Claude Code throughout this project. It wrote most of the Python (the `/submit` and `/appeal` routes, `stylometry.py`, `scoring.py::combine_signals`, and the scripts that formatted my test files). I wrote my own acceptance criteria and the three label texts, chose the thresholds, and decided what to do when my results came back bad.

**Moment 1: checking my criteria**

- *What I asked for:* I asked how to think about acceptance criteria and what a check actually takes in and gives back. Then I wrote five criteria myself and pasted them in, and asked whether someone else could test them.
- *What came back:* It said the criteria were mostly checkable, but listed problems: I used "Appeal pending" while the starter uses `under_review`; my test sets had no named location; criterion 3 only tested controlled scores, so a label could be unreachable on real text; the appeal criterion had no case for a fake `content_id`.
- *What I changed:* I kept all my numbers (0.4 gap, 0.2 and 0.9 cutoffs, 20,000 characters). I switched to `under_review`, named `tests/human.jsonl` and `tests/ai.jsonl`, and added the reachability line and the 404 case. I committed `criteria.md` before scoring anything.

**Moment 2: my first real results**

- *What I asked for:* After building the second signal, I asked it to run both signals over my 20 human and 10 AI pieces and print one table.
- *What came back:* The signals barely separated them. Human median was 0.565 and AI median 0.536 on signal one, and casual human posts scored very AI-like. It also showed my 0.2 and 0.9 cutoffs were not reachable with real text, and gave me three options: keep the criteria and report the misses, amend the thresholds, or tune the signals.
- *What I changed:* I kept my criteria as written and set `AI_THRESHOLD` to 0.9, so no human in my test set gets an AI label, even though that means no real input reaches the AI label. I also set the human cutoff at 0.35 so that label stays reachable, and I recorded both differences in the README. For the labels, the AI drafted wording first, and I replaced it with my own.
