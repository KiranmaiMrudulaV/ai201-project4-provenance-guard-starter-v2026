## What This Does

A writer's text goes through seven steps before a reader sees a label.

1. **Arrives.** `POST /submit` receives JSON with `text` and `creator_id` (`app.py::submit`).
2. **Validated.** Missing, empty, or non-string `text` or `creator_id` returns HTTP 400. Text over 20,000 characters returns HTTP 413. Rejected requests write nothing to the log.
3. **Signal one.** `detector.model_signal` asks a local gpt2 model how predictable the text is and returns a 0–1 score (higher = more AI-like).
4. **Signal two.** `stylometry.style_signal` measures the shape of the text (sentence-length spread, word variety, punctuation) and returns a 0–1 score in the same direction.
5. **Combined.** `scoring.combine_signals` turns the two scores into one number.
6. **Labelled.** `scoring.score_to_label` maps that number to one of three labels and a short `guess` (`ai`, `human`, `unsure`).
7. **Logged and answered.** `audit.log_decision` writes both signal scores, the combined score, the label and a `decided` status to `logs/audit.jsonl`, and the response returns the same fields plus a `content_id`.

If a writer disagrees, `POST /appeal` takes the `content_id` and their reasoning, writes the appeal to the log next to the original decision, and moves the item to `under_review`. An unknown `content_id` returns HTTP 404 and writes nothing.

## Signals and Scoring

### Signal one — the model signal

**What it measures:** how predictable the text is to a small language model (gpt2), as perplexity, scaled to a 0–1 score. File: `detector.py`.

**Why that might differ between human and AI writing:** text generated by a language model tends to use the most likely next word, so another model finds it easy to predict. Human writing tends to surprise the model more.

**What it can't see:** meaning, intent, or who wrote it. It only sees predictability, so plain, everyday human writing also looks predictable. In my test sets, casual human posts scored very AI-like (h15 0.83, h12 0.80, h10 0.78), while rhymed AI poems scored low (a09 0.18, a07 0.29). It is also noisy on short texts of about 80–100 words.

### Signal two — the style signal

**What it measures:** the shape of the text, without reading it. File: `stylometry.py`. It combines three measures, each scaled to 0–1 and flipped so that higher means more AI-like: sentence-length spread (standard deviation divided by mean, weight 0.4), type-token ratio over the first 100 words (weight 0.3), and punctuation marks per word (weight 0.3). A single sentence has no spread, so that measure is skipped.

**Why that might differ between human and AI writing:** generated prose tends toward even, mid-length sentences, a narrower vocabulary, and tidy punctuation. Human writing is lumpier.

**What it can't see:** what the text says. Anything that changes the shape without changing the substance moves it: typos, chopped-up sentences, pasted quotations. Poems are also a weak spot, because the sentence splitter looks for full stops and poem lines often have none. In my test sets it barely separated the groups (AI pieces 0.39–0.52, human pieces 0.26–0.58).

### The combining rule

**The rule:** a weighted average of the two scores. If the two signals differ by more than 0.35, the result is pulled halfway back toward 0.5. Two methods disagreeing is itself uncertainty, so an "AI" verdict needs both signals leaning the same way. The stretch phrase signal (see below) is then added on top as a one-way nudge of at most +0.25. I chose this to protect writers: a wrongly flagged human costs more than a missed AI piece.

**The numbers:** model weight 0.5, style weight 0.5, disagreement gap 0.35, shrink factor 0.5, phrase bonus 0.25 (all in `config.py`). Label cutoffs: human at or below 0.35, AI at or above 0.90.

**Where it lives:** `scoring.py::combine_signals`

**A case where my two signals split, and what my rule does with it:** piece h10, a casual post asking for movie recommendations (human-written). Signal one gave 0.781 and signal two gave 0.329, a gap of 0.45. A plain average is 0.555. Because the gap is over 0.35 the result is pulled to about 0.53, firmly "unsure" instead of leaning AI.

### What my test sets showed

I wrote my criteria first (`criteria.md`, committed before any scores), then scored 20 human pieces (`tests/human.jsonl`) and 10 AI pieces (`tests/ai.jsonl`).

- Signal one: human median 0.565, AI median 0.536. Signal two: human 0.423, AI 0.457. Combined scores (two signals) ran from about 0.28 to 0.70; the third signal's effect is under Stretch 1.
- **Criterion 1 (no human labelled AI): met, but trivially.** With the cutoff at 0.9, no piece of either kind reaches the AI label.
- **Criterion 2 (median gap of 0.4): missed by a wide margin.** The combined medians were about 0.48 for human and 0.48 for AI. The two signals together do not separate these samples.
- **Criterion 3 (label coverage): partly missed.** The mapping works at the boundaries, but no real input reaches the AI label. I also set the human cutoff at 0.35, not the 0.2 written in the criterion, because the lowest real score was about 0.28 and 0.2 would make the human label unreachable too.
- **Criteria 4 and 5 (appeal path, bad input): met in testing.** Appeals set `under_review` and log the previous status; an unknown id returns 404; missing text returns 400; 20,001 characters returns 413 and 20,000 passes.

Caveat: all three human poems (Eliot, Donne, Frost) are famous and may be memorized by gpt2, and all four AI poems are rhymed quatrains, so the poem comparison is weak.

## Label Variants

| Label | Score range | The exact text a reader sees |
|---|---|---|
| high-confidence human | 0.00 – 0.35 | This text may be human-written, but we cannot verify the authorship. No further action is required. |
| unsure | 0.35 – 0.90 | We could not determine whether AI was used. No action is required, and the writer can request a review. |
| high-confidence AI | 0.90 – 1.00 | This text may be AI-generated, but this can be wrong. The writer can request a review. |

**Why I worded the "unsure" one this way:** it does not accuse anyone and it says plainly that the system could not decide. It also tells the writer they can ask for a review, because the person reading it may have written every word themselves. Even the "human" label says it cannot verify authorship, because the system cannot prove it either way.

## Stretch Features (declared before building)

I am adding all three stretch features. This section was committed before any of them were built; what each one did is written up in the subsections below once it exists.

1. **A third signal.** A phrase signal that measures how many stock chatbot phrases a text uses ("plays a pivotal role", "ultimately", "in today's fast-paced world"). It is meant to measure something different from predictability (signal one) and shape (signal two). I will write down how the three signals are weighted and why.
2. **`GET /content/<id>`.** Returns one item's current status, label and scores.
3. **A verified-human path.** A writer earns a credential through an extra step (answering a challenge prompt in their own words), and the credential shows on their work in the response and the log.

### Stretch 1 — the third signal (`phrases.py::phrase_signal`)

**What it measures:** how many stock chatbot phrases a text contains ("plays a pivotal role", "ultimately", "delve", "tapestry" and about 30 others), per 100 words. A density of 3 hits per 100 words scores 1.0. The list was written from general knowledge of chatbot habits, not tuned on my test pieces.

**What it can't see:** anything not on the list. A model told to avoid those phrases walks past it, and a formal human writer can trip it. Poems and casual writing score about zero whoever wrote them, so it can only ever argue for "AI".

**How the three are weighted, and why:** signals one and two are averaged 0.5/0.5 as before (with the disagreement rule). The phrase signal is **not** averaged in. It scored 0 on 26 of my 30 test pieces, and averaging in a zero would drag every text toward "human" whenever the list didn't match. Instead it is added on top as a one-way nudge: `score += 0.25 × phrase_score`, capped at 1.0. Absence says nothing; a hit is evidence. The weight of 0.25 is my judgment, not a fitted value.

**What it changed:** on my test sets the phrase signal fired on 4 of 10 AI pieces and 2 of 20 human ones. Both human hits were false alarms (h13 uses "Furthermore" in a rant, and h22, the Frost poem, says "harness bells"). With it, the combined medians moved from 0.477 (AI) and 0.482 (human) to 0.569 (AI) and 0.517 (human). That is a gap of about 0.05, far short of criterion 2's 0.4, and the highest AI piece is still only 0.77, below the 0.9 cutoff. It helps a little and does not fix the problem.

### Stretch 3 — the verified-human path

**The step:** a writer asks for a challenge (`POST /verify/challenge`) and gets a prompt with a random word in it. They answer it in their own words (`POST /verify`), at least 30 words and including the random word. A passing answer records a `verification` event in the audit log. From then on every submission by that `creator_id` carries `"verified_human": true` in the response, in the log entry, and in `GET /content/<id>`. A short answer returns 422; an unknown or mismatched challenge returns 404.

**What it is not:** a light check that someone can write a live answer, not proof of authorship. It does not change the score or the label; it only shows on the work.

## Rate Limiting

**My limits:** 3 per minute, 30 per day, on `POST /submit` (`config.py`, enforced by the `@limiter.limit(...)` line in `app.py`).

**Why those numbers and not others:** a real writer submits their own work a few times an hour. Even when they revise and resubmit, each check takes a second or two and they stop to read the result, so 3 in a minute is a normal burst and 30 in a day is generous. A script mapping my thresholds needs hundreds of probes, so the daily cap of 30 is what hurts it most; the per-minute cap slows its bursts. I tried 2 per minute and 20 per day first and a 15-request flood was blocked almost immediately, which was tighter than I wanted because it would also block a writer sending two quick revisions. I settled on 3 and 30 as the point where a normal burst still gets through and a flood does not. These are my judgment, not measured values.

**What a caller counts as:** the `creator_id` in the request, falling back to the caller's address when there is none (`app.py::rate_limit_key`). Per creator means one script using many addresses still hits the same limit, but a script that rotates `creator_id` values gets a fresh allowance each time, and one household sharing an address is not penalised.

**Diagnoses**

*Criterion 2 missed — stages: signal one, signal two, and the combining rule.* Signal one's medians were almost equal (human 0.565, AI 0.536 on my test sets) because gpt2 finds plain, casual human writing predictable (h15 scored 0.83, h12 0.80) while rhymed AI poems scored low (a09 0.18, a07 0.29). Signal two barely separates them either: AI pieces sat at 0.39–0.52, inside the human range of 0.26–0.58. My combining rule averages the two at 0.5 each, so two weak signals give a weak result, and the phrase bonus (+0.25 only when a stock phrase appears) lifted only 4 of 10 AI pieces.

*Criterion 3 missed — stages: label mapping and combining rule.* Two separate mechanisms. First, the label mapping: `config.HUMAN_THRESHOLD` is 0.35, not the 0.2 written in my criterion, so a score of 0.2 returns "human" instead of "unsure"; I set 0.35 so that the human label was reachable at all, and never rewrote the criterion. Second, the AI label is unreachable on real text: the combining rule averages signals that sit mid-range, so real scores landed between 0.28 and 0.77, and `config.AI_THRESHOLD` of 0.9 is above every one of them.

*MF02 broke (a one-character text returned a 500) — stage: the route, with signal one.* `app.py::submit` only checks that the text is non-empty after stripping, then calls `detector.model_signal`, which raises `ValueError: Text is too short to score` for a single character. Nothing catches it, so Flask returns an HTML error page, and because the crash happens before the route logs anything, it left no audit entry.

*MF06 and MF07 broke (emoji-only and right-to-left text labelled AI, combined 0.9985 and 0.9532) — stage: signal two, amplified by signal one.* `stylometry.words()` matches only `[a-z0-9']`, so these texts contain zero words. With no words, the type-token ratio and punctuation density both read as maximally AI-like, giving a style score of 1.0, and signal one also gave a high score (0.9969 and 0.9064). Because both signals leaned the same way, my disagreement rule never fired, and the average cleared the 0.9 cutoff.

*EV05 broke (heavy human edit got the human label) — stage: signals one and two together.* After the editing pass the text read as less predictable to signal one (0.321) and lumpier to signal two (0.304). They agreed, so the disagreement rule gave no protection, and the combined 0.313 fell under the 0.35 human cutoff.

**Pattern across the misses:** the two signals do not separate real human from AI text in my sets, which compresses scores into about 0.28–0.77 and makes the thresholds close to arbitrary (criteria 2 and 3, EV05). Separately, both signals treat "no usable words" as AI (MF06, MF07), and the route has an input gap that criterion 5 did not cover (MF02).

## The Improvement

**What I changed:** one thing. `app.py::submit` now refuses text with fewer than 5 Latin-alphabet words, before either signal sees it (`config.MIN_WORDS = 5`). It returns HTTP 400 with the error `unscorable_text` and an explanation, and writes a `rejected` entry to the audit log (reason `too_few_words`). Commit `68b4a94`. Nothing else was touched: not the signals, weights, thresholds or labels. I counted the words with the same `stylometry.words()` function signal two uses, and I picked 5 because the shortest genuine human message in the attack set (FP08) has 8 words and the next shortest (EV08) has 10, while every unscorable attack has 3 words or fewer.

**Which diagnosis pointed at it:** three of my four broken attacks came from one cause. MF02 (a single character) crashed in signal one because the route passed it through unchecked. MF06 (emoji) and MF07 (right-to-left script) were labelled AI because signal two finds zero words in them and scores that as maximally AI-like. All three are text the signals cannot read, so the fix is to stop it before the signals.

## What's Still Broken

- **EV05 (heavy human edit still gets the human label).** Both signals read the edited text as human and they agree, so my disagreement rule has nothing to catch. I would need a signal that is not moved by an editing pass, or to lower the human cutoff so an agreement this weak stays "unsure". I stopped because either is a second change, and the cutoff move trades against criterion 3.
- **Criterion 2 (gap 0.063 against 0.4).** The signals do not separate my test sets. A larger detector model or a calibration step against labelled data is the real fix. I stopped because it is far bigger than one change and the improvement rule allowed one.
- **Criterion 3 (AI label unreachable; 0.2 versus 0.35).** No real piece reaches the 0.9 AI cutoff, and the human cutoff in `config.py` is 0.35 where my criterion says 0.2. I would set both cutoffs from measured data. I stopped because lowering the AI cutoff to something reachable, about 0.65, would put a human piece (h15, 0.704) in the AI band, which breaks criterion 1.
- **Criterion 5 (exactly 20,000 characters).** I would apply the 5-word minimum only to short text, or count tokens instead of words, and re-run. I stopped because that is a second change on top of the one measured here.
- **Non-Latin text is refused, not understood.** The real fix is a Unicode-aware word splitter in signal two and a detector that handles other scripts. I stopped because it changes a signal.
- **Rate limiting is per `creator_id`,** so a script that rotates the id gets a fresh allowance each time. I did not attempt per-address limits on top.
- **Prompt injection held, but only by architecture.** Neither signal reads instructions, so the six attacks changed nothing, but that protection would vanish if I added a model that reads the text as a prompt.

**The trade I'd make if this ran for real:** I would keep the 0.9 AI cutoff, because a wrong accusation damages a real writer and a missed AI piece only costs the site some moderation. The people who pay for that are the site's readers and any writer whose honest work is competing with undetected AI text, since with these signals the site would almost never say "AI" at all.
